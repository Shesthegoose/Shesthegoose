Cedar-Plank Salmon with Fresh Thyme

An hour of soaking, twenty minutes of standing there, and dinner cooks itself.

22 min + soakServes 4–6Grill 375–400°F

Soaking the plank is the only demanding step. Glaze once before cooking — glazing over the fire turns the honey bitter.

Ingredients

The fish

  • 1 large salmon fillet, skin on, about 2 lb
  • 1 untreated cedar plank

The glaze

  • 2 Tbsp olive oil
  • 2 Tbsp honey
  • 1½ Tbsp Dijon mustard
  • 1 Tbsp soy sauce
  • 2 cloves garlic, finely minced
  • 1½ Tbsp fresh thyme leaves
  • 1 tsp finely grated lemon zest
  • 1 Tbsp fresh lemon juice
  • ½ tsp kosher salt
  • ¼ tsp freshly ground black pepper

To finish

  • 1 Tbsp unsalted butter, melted
  • Fresh thyme sprigs and thin lemon slices, optional

Method

  1. 01

    Submerge the plank in water at least 1 hour, preferably 2, weighted down so it stays under.

  2. 02

    Whisk together the olive oil, honey, Dijon, soy sauce, garlic, thyme leaves, lemon zest, lemon juice, salt, and pepper.

  3. 03

    Pat the salmon dry and set it skin-side down on the soaked plank. Brush it generously with the glaze and let it stand 15–20 minutes while the grill heats.

  4. 04

    Heat the grill to 375–400°F. Set the plank over indirect heat, close the lid, and cook 15–22 minutes depending on the thickness of the fillet. For a moist center, take it off around 125–130°F at the thickest part and let it rest briefly.

  5. 05

    Brush the hot salmon very lightly with the melted butter and scatter fresh thyme over the top. A small squeeze of lemon just before serving.

Notes

Use only untreated cedar planks sold for grilling — never treated lumber. The plank is single-use.

For presentation, add the thyme sprigs and lemon slices during the last five minutes so they don’t scorch. Keep a spray bottle nearby for plank edges that catch flame.
