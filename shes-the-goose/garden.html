Garden

What is happening outside, month by month, in one ordinary garden in New England.

The long border in the second week of August, from the lawn side. Planted in threes and fives, fed twice a season, and otherwise left to get on with it.

Spring

March – May

Everything in this bed went in over one afternoon in late March, in the cold, badly. It is still the highest-return hour of the whole garden year — and the only work that has to happen before the season decides for you.

Early Summer

June

June is for containers. Plant them roughly twice as full as looks sensible — by the second week of August they will be exactly right, and by September they will be generous.

High Summer

July – mid-August

Nothing in this border is rare. It is all garden-centre stock, planted densely, fed twice, and cut back once in July. Density is doing the work that a plant list is usually given credit for.

Late Summer

Mid-August – September

The garden stops asking for much after the middle of August. This is the month to walk it rather than work it, and to write down what should move in October.

Letter no. 13 · Summer

The Long Light

It was almost nine last night and still light out. I checked the clock twice because it didn’t seem possible. The dishes were in the sink where I’d left them. I sat down on the back step instead and never did go in.
